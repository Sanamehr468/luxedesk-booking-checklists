## How to use these checklists

Copy a checklist into your notes app, planner or LuxeDesk workflow, tick items off as you go, and adapt the wording to your own services. Each checklist is written so a solo owner or a small team can use it without extra software.

---

## (a) New Client Intake Checklist

Use this checklist the first time a client books, so you have everything you need before they arrive.

### Contact details

- [ ] Full name and preferred name
- [ ] Phone number and email address
- [ ] Preferred contact method (call, text, email or WhatsApp)
- [ ] How they found you (referral, search, social media, walk-in)
- [ ] Emergency contact, where relevant

### Allergies, health and preferences

- [ ] Allergies and sensitivities noted (for example, hair colour, skincare or nail products)
- [ ] Relevant health conditions or medications the client wishes to share
- [ ] Skin, hair or nail history that affects treatment choice
- [ ] Preferences: style, scent, pressure, products, music, conversation level
- [ ] Things to avoid, recorded in the client's own words

### Colour formulas and service notes

- [ ] Service requested and agreed outcome
- [ ] Colour formula used, including brand, shade, developer and mix ratio
- [ ] Application notes: timing, technique and any adjustments
- [ ] Before-and-after notes or photos, with the client's permission
- [ ] Products recommended and products used at home
- [ ] Next appointment suggested and booked, where appropriate

### Consent

- [ ] Intake form completed and signed before treatment
- [ ] Patch test offered or completed, where required
- [ ] Risks, aftercare and expected results explained in plain language
- [ ] Consent to store contact details and service notes confirmed
- [ ] Consent for photos or marketing use confirmed separately — never assume it
- [ ] Client signature and date recorded, and a copy offered to the client

> Tip: Keep intake notes consistent. When a returning client books, check their last formula and allergy notes before you confirm the appointment.

## (b) Booking Day Checklist

Use this checklist every working day to keep appointments on track and reduce no-shows.

### Confirm

- [ ] Review today's appointments, gaps and double-bookings
- [ ] Confirm each booking with the client (message, call or booking-page confirmation)
- [ ] Check that the service, time, practitioner and price are correct
- [ ] Flag any first-time clients who still need an intake form
- [ ] Confirm staff, rooms and equipment for the day

### Remind

- [ ] Send a reminder the day before, with date, time, location and what to bring
- [ ] Include arrival time, parking or access notes, and how to reschedule
- [ ] Send a same-day reminder for early or high-value appointments
- [ ] Follow up any unconfirmed bookings promptly — do not leave them to chance

### Prepare

- [ ] Prepare the workstation, tools and products for each appointment
- [ ] Check stock for the day's services and reorder anything running low
- [ ] Review client notes: allergies, preferences, colour formulas and previous feedback
- [ ] Set out intake or consent forms for new clients
- [ ] Allow buffer time between appointments for cleaning and notes

### No-show follow-up

- [ ] Note no-shows and late cancellations on the client's record
- [ ] Message the client the same day: check they are well and offer to rebook
- [ ] Offer the next available slot, rather than leaving rebooking to the client
- [ ] Apply your cancellation policy fairly and consistently, and explain it politely
- [ ] Review no-show patterns weekly and adjust reminders or deposits if needed

> Tip: A shareable booking page, like the one in LuxeDesk, cuts down on back-and-forth messages because clients can see real availability and book directly.

## (c) Invoice Checklist

Use this checklist for every invoice, so clients are charged correctly and you get paid on time.

### Branded invoice

- [ ] Business name, logo, address and contact details on the invoice
- [ ] Client name and contact details, spelled correctly
- [ ] Unique invoice number and invoice date
- [ ] Clear description of each service or product, with quantity and price
- [ ] Taxes, discounts and totals shown separately and added up correctly
- [ ] Payment methods, payment terms and due date stated clearly
- [ ] A polite thank-you note and a note about how to query the invoice

### Paid, pending and overdue tracking

- [ ] Mark each invoice as paid, pending or overdue as soon as its status changes
- [ ] Record the payment date, method and amount for paid invoices
- [ ] Send a friendly reminder shortly before a pending invoice is due
- [ ] Follow up overdue invoices in stages: reminder, firmer reminder, then a direct call
- [ ] Keep a note of every follow-up, so nothing is chased twice or missed
- [ ] Reconcile invoices against bank or payment records each week

### Monthly goals

- [ ] Set a monthly income goal based on bookings, not just hope
- [ ] Track invoices issued, paid and overdue against that goal
- [ ] Review your busiest services and most reliable clients each month
- [ ] Spot seasonal patterns and plan promotions or quiet-week tasks around them
- [ ] Set one improvement for next month — for example, faster invoicing or fewer overdue invoices
- [ ] Back up invoice records and keep them organised for tax time

> Tip: Sending the invoice on the day of the appointment, while the service is fresh in the client's mind, usually leads to faster payment than invoicing at the end of the month.
